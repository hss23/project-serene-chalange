# How Matching Works

## Overview of the matching process
Matches are suggested by a transparent scoring algorithm and then confirmed by people. A mentee selects their profile, sees a ranked shortlist of up to five mentors with a score out of 100 and the reasons behind it, and requests an introduction to one mentor. The mentor has seven days to accept or decline.

## Scoring factors and weights
The match score combines six factors. Skill fit is worth 40 percent and measures how many of the mentee's goal skills the mentor covers, weighted by how important each skill is to the mentee and how proficient the mentor is. Availability overlap is worth 15 percent. Experience is worth 15 percent and compares the mentor's years of experience with the minimum the mentee asked for. Timezone closeness is worth 10 percent and drops to zero at eight or more hours apart. Industry match is worth 10 percent. Session format and shared language together are worth the final 10 percent.

## Weak matches
Any suggested match with a score below 40 is labelled as a weak match. Weak matches are still shown so that mentees in niche fields have options, but mentees are encouraged to broaden their goal skills or availability to improve their results.

## Improving your matches
The quickest ways to improve match quality are to list three to five specific goal skills, mark which ones are must-haves, and add more availability slots. Adding every language you are comfortable speaking also helps, because a shared language counts toward the score.

## Declined requests
If a mentor declines an introduction request, the mentee can immediately request a different mentor from their shortlist. A decline is usually about the mentor's capacity rather than the mentee, and it is not shown on the mentee's profile.
