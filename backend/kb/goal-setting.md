# Goal Setting Guide

## Why goals matter
Mentorships with written goals are more focused and easier to evaluate at the end of a cycle. Every mentee writes up to three goals during the first two sessions and reviews them with their mentor at the midpoint and at the end of the cycle.

## Writing SMART goals
Goals should be specific, measurable, achievable, relevant and time-bound. For example, "get better at system design" becomes "complete three mock system design interviews with written feedback by week 10". Each goal should have one clear way to tell whether it has been met.

## Midpoint review
In week six, the pair holds a midpoint review. They check progress on each goal, decide whether any goal should be changed or dropped, and agree on the focus for the second half of the cycle. Changing goals at the midpoint is normal and is not counted as failure.

## End-of-cycle reflection
In the final session the mentee presents a short reflection: which goals were met, what they learned, and what they want to work on next. Mentees receive a digital certificate of participation after submitting the end-of-cycle feedback form.

## Example goals by track
Engineering mentees often choose goals such as leading a design review, improving test coverage on a project, or preparing for a senior interview loop. Design mentees often choose building a portfolio case study or running five user interviews. Product mentees often choose writing a product strategy document or presenting a roadmap to stakeholders.
